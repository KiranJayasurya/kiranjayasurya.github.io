HIGH-ENERGY ASTROPHYSICS · X-RAY ASTRONOMY · X-RAY INSTRUMENTATION

Kiran M. Jayasurya

I study accretion onto compact objects through X-ray spectroscopy, timing, and polarimetry.

My research focuses on accreting neutron stars, ultraluminous X-ray sources, and the physics of matter in extreme magnetic fields and radiation environments.

01 RESEARCH

Research interests

I use high-energy observations and instrumentation to investigate accretion, emission geometry, and energetic transients.

R / 01

X-ray binaries and ultraluminous X-ray sources

Accretion onto neutron stars and black holes, including super-Eddington flows, outflows, and ultraluminous X-ray sources.

Accretion physicsULXs

R / 02

X-ray spectro-polarimetry

Using polarization, spectroscopy, and timing to constrain the geometry of accretion columns, scattering regions, and outflows.

IXPEPolarimetry

R / 03

X-ray instrumentation

Development and simulation of broadband X-ray spectrometers and photoelectric polarimeters, including track reconstruction for gas detectors.

XPoSatTPC polarimeters

R / 04

High-energy transients

Earlier research interests include tidal disruption events around supermassive black holes and gamma-ray burst afterglows.

03 ABOUT

About

Research, instrumentation, and astrophysics.

I am a Scientist in the Space Astronomy Group at the U R Rao Satellite Centre (URSC), ISRO, Bengaluru, and a PhD researcher at the Raman Research Institute (RRI).

My current work is centred on high-energy astrophysics, especially X-ray spectro-polarimetry of accreting compact objects and the development of X-ray astronomy instrumentation. My earlier research included tidal disruption events, gamma-ray burst afterglows, and accretion disks around compact objects.
